2. What Business Problem Does This Solve?

Banks and lenders need to decide whether a borrower is likely to repay a loan. A simple model can say default or not default, but a real decision system needs more:

  • default probability: how risky is the applicant?
  • risk score: a scorecard-style number for business users
  • risk band: low, medium, high or very high risk
  • decision: approve, manual review or reject
  • explanation: which factors are influencing risk?

The project follows this flow:

borrower data -> cleaning -> ML model -> default probability
default probability -> risk score -> risk band -> credit decision -> dashboard explanation
Simple answer: I upgraded a loan default prediction model into a small credit-risk decision system that is easier for a finance team to understand and use.

5. Data Cleaning and Leakage Removal

What is the target?

The target is default, where:

  • 0 means non-default / safer loan, such as fully paid.
  • 1 means default / high-risk loan, such as charged off, default, late or not paid.

convert_target_to_binary() (src/project_utils.py) handles numeric labels, yes/no labels, fully paid, charged off, default, late and current loans. Current/grace loans are removed because their final outcome is not known yet.

What is leakage?

Data leakage means using information that would not be available at the time of prediction. In credit risk, post-loan outcome columns can accidentally reveal whether the borrower defaulted.

Example leakage-like fields:

  • recovery amount after default
  • collection status
  • last payment related fields
  • settlement or hardship information
  • loan status itself as a feature
Why leakage removal matters: If leakage columns remain, the model may look very accurate but it is cheating. It would fail on real new applicants.

clean_raw_data() (src/project_utils.py) drops leakage, ID-like and constant columns, then keeps useful borrower-level features where available.

6. Model Training and Evaluation

Preprocessing pipeline

create_pipeline() (src/train_model.py) builds one complete Scikit-learn pipeline:

  • Numeric columns: median imputation + standard scaling.
  • Categorical columns: most-frequent imputation + one-hot encoding.
  • Model: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree or Random Forest.

This is important because the same preprocessing is used during training and prediction.

Models trained

train_test_split() (src/train_model.py) uses 80% training and 20% testing with random_state=42. Stratification keeps the default/non-default ratio similar in both sets.

Metrics

evaluate_model() (src/train_model.py) calculates accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score and ROC-AUC.

7. Risk Score, Risk Band and Decision Rules

From probability to credit score

probability_to_score() (src/project_utils.py) converts default probability into a score:

risk_score = 850 - default_probability * 550

Higher default probability gives lower score. This makes the model output easier for a finance user to understand.

Risk bands

probability_to_risk_band() (src/project_utils.py) uses:

Default probabilityRisk band
< 0.20Low Risk
0.20 to < 0.40Medium Risk
0.40 to < 0.60High Risk
>= 0.60Very High Risk

Lending decision

probability_to_decision() (src/project_utils.py) uses:

Default probabilityDecision
< 0.25Approve
0.25 to < 0.50Manual Review
>= 0.50Reject
Important: These are simple business rules for a project demo. In real lending, thresholds must be selected using business cost, regulation, fairness checks and historical validation.

Threshold analysis

create_threshold_table() (src/train_model.py) tests thresholds 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60. A lower threshold catches more defaults but flags more safe borrowers. A higher threshold approves more borrowers but may miss risky loans.

10. ML Concepts You Must Know

Classification

This is a binary classification problem because the target has two classes: default = 1 and non-default = 0.

Probability vs class label

The model first predicts probability, such as 0.3729. A threshold converts it into class 0/1. Business rules convert it into score, band and decision.

Why ROC-AUC?

ROC-AUC measures ranking quality. In credit risk, ranking borrowers from safest to riskiest is very useful because the threshold can be adjusted later depending on business risk appetite.

Why Logistic Regression performed well?

Credit risk data often has strong linear risk signals. Logistic Regression with scaling and class weights can perform strongly, especially after leakage removal. It is also easier to explain than complex models.

Why class_weight="balanced"?

Defaults are fewer than non-defaults. Balanced class weights tell the model to pay more attention to the minority default class.

Feature importance

For Logistic Regression, coefficients show direction and strength. For trees, feature_importances_ shows which variables were used most to reduce uncertainty.

11. Interview Questions With Answers

Q1. Explain the project in 30 seconds.

I built a credit-risk scoring system on LendingClub-style loan data. It cleans raw loan records, removes leakage, trains classification models, selects the best model by ROC-AUC, and converts default probability into risk score, risk band and lending decision. I also built a Streamlit dashboard for risk analytics, threshold tradeoffs, feature importance and applicant-level scoring.

Q2. What is data leakage?

Data leakage means using information that would not be available at prediction time. In loan data, post-loan variables like collections, recoveries or final payment information can reveal the outcome. I removed such columns so the model learns from borrower-level information instead of cheating.

Q3. Why did you use ROC-AUC?

ROC-AUC evaluates how well the model ranks risky borrowers above safer borrowers across thresholds. Since lending decisions may use different thresholds depending on business policy, ROC-AUC is more useful than only accuracy.

Q4. What does a ROC-AUC of 0.7398 mean?

It means the model has useful ranking power. If we randomly take one defaulted and one non-defaulted borrower, the model will rank the defaulted one as riskier around 74% of the time. It is useful but not perfect.

Q5. Why not only show default/not-default?

A binary label hides business nuance. A probability lets us create risk bands, manual review ranges, and threshold tradeoff analysis. That makes the project more like a decision-support system.

Q6. What is threshold analysis?

Threshold analysis shows what happens when we change the probability cutoff. Lower thresholds catch more risky borrowers but also reject more safe borrowers. Higher thresholds approve more borrowers but may miss more defaults.

Q7. How does the applicant scoring form work?

The form uses the saved input schema to collect feature values in the correct order. The saved pipeline preprocesses the input, predicts default probability, and then utility functions convert probability into score, band and decision.

Q8. Is this production-ready?

No. It is a learning and portfolio project. A production credit model would need validation on fresh data, fairness checks, explainability review, monitoring, security and regulatory approval.
